# US Housing — First-Time Buyer Reference

A framework for the rent-vs-buy decision and a comparison of common relocation targets. Specific rates and prices move weekly; treat anything numeric here as a placeholder to refresh before acting.

## Mortgage rate dynamics

- **30-year fixed** is the US default. Rate tracks the 10-year Treasury plus a spread (historically 150–200 bp, wider in stressed markets).
- **15-year fixed**: lower rate, larger payment, faster equity build — rarely optimal unless cash flow is strong.
- **ARMs (5/1, 7/1, 10/1)**: lower initial rate, resets after the fixed period. Right only if you're confident you'll sell or refinance before reset.
- **Points**: upfront fee to lower the rate. Break-even depends on how long you'll hold.
- **Conforming vs jumbo**: loan size above the FHFA conforming limit gets a different rate. Limit differs by county.
- **FHA**: 3.5% down if credit allows; mortgage insurance for life of the loan post-2013.
- **Conventional with PMI**: as little as 3–5% down, PMI drops off at 78–80% LTV.

A 100 bp rise in the 30-year reduces affordable home price by roughly 10% at fixed monthly payment. That's why housing demand is rate-sensitive even though housing supply is not.

## First-time buyer cost beyond the down payment

- **Closing costs**: 2–5% of purchase price. Origination, title, recording, inspection, appraisal, pre-paid taxes + insurance.
- **Moving and setup**: 0.5–2% of purchase price in practice.
- **Maintenance reserve**: rule of thumb 1% of home value per year. Older homes run higher.
- **HOA**: if applicable, can add $200–$800/month; review HOA financials before buying — special assessments are a killer.
- **Property tax + homeowner's insurance**: big state-by-state variance.

## Rent vs buy — the real math

Simple version: buying beats renting over the long run if **home appreciation + imputed rent + principal paydown − maintenance − interest − taxes** exceeds the return on the **equivalent portfolio** of your down payment and monthly surplus.

Rules of thumb, calibrated to current rate levels:

- **Price-to-rent ratio** < 15 in an area = buy is usually favourable; > 20 = rent tends to win; 15–20 is personal fit.
- **Hold period**: breakeven is rarely below 5 years with closing costs and taxes. If you might move in 3, rent.
- **Flexibility premium**: renting has a real option value if your job / visa / life is in flux. Don't ignore it.

## Georgia, specifically

Relevant because SAP's US HQ is in Alpharetta and many SAP jobs are Atlanta-anchored. Considerations:

- **Metro Atlanta** price-to-rent ratios vary from ~14 (outer suburbs) to ~20 (intown). Buying is more favourable in the suburbs; renting is often better intown.
- **Commute**: metro Atlanta is car-dependent outside of a few rail-adjacent pockets.
- **Climate**: humid summers, mild winters; HVAC costs are significant.
- **Transfer tax**: Georgia's transfer tax is low by US standards (~$1 per $1,000).

## Before making an offer

1. Get pre-approved, not pre-qualified.
2. Model the monthly payment at rate + 100 bp — would you still buy?
3. Budget 3–5% for closing, 1% for maintenance annually, and a 6-month reserve.
4. Walk the area at three different times (weekday morning, weekend evening, Friday night).
5. Do an independent inspection. Always.
