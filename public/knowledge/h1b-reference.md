# H1B and STEM OPT Reference

A framework-level reference for US work visas. Specific rules and fees change — always cross-check USCIS and a qualified attorney for anything consequential.

## The pathway at a glance

For a student graduating in the US in a STEM field, the usual path is:

**F-1 → OPT (1 year) → STEM OPT (2 years) → H-1B (up to 6 years) → EB-2/EB-3 green card**

Each stage has its own timing risks and employer dependencies.

## OPT and STEM OPT

- **OPT** (Optional Practical Training): 12 months of work authorisation after completing a degree. F-1 status continues.
- **STEM OPT extension**: an additional 24 months for graduates in designated STEM fields, employer must be E-Verify enrolled and sign a Form I-983 training plan.
- You can accrue up to **90 days of unemployment** during initial OPT, and an additional 60 days during the 24-month STEM extension. Exceeding it risks status.
- Employer changes require updates to USCIS within 10 days.

The clock for STEM OPT starts on its own EAD approval date, not at graduation.

## H-1B

- **Cap**: 65,000 regular + 20,000 US master's or higher = 85,000/year. Way more demand than supply, so there's a lottery.
- **Lottery timing**: electronic registration opens in March; lottery selection happens mid-March; if selected, the petition window opens April 1 with an October 1 start date.
- **Validity**: 3 years initially, renewable for 3 more → 6 years total. Extensions beyond 6 years possible if a green-card process is in progress.
- **Cap-gap**: if your F-1 OPT expires after April 1 but before October 1, and you have a pending/approved H-1B petition, status and work authorisation **extend automatically** to September 30 (the "cap-gap extension"). If OPT expires before April 1, there's no cap-gap — you lose work auth in the interim.
- **Transfer**: you can change H-1B employers with a new petition; the old petition ends when the new one is approved. The lottery isn't re-run.

## From H-1B to green card

Two common employment-based categories:

- **EB-2**: advanced degree or "exceptional ability" — typically for roles requiring a master's or PhD.
- **EB-3**: skilled workers with a bachelor's or 2+ years of experience.

The process:

1. **PERM labour certification** (DOL): the employer proves no qualified US worker is available. ~6–18 months.
2. **I-140 petition** (USCIS): employer petitions for the worker. Premium processing available.
3. **Priority date wait**: for applicants chargeable to India or China (by country of birth), the wait can be many years due to per-country caps. Check the Visa Bulletin monthly.
4. **I-485 (adjustment of status)** or consular processing: final step, you become a permanent resident.

Job changes during the I-485 pending phase are possible under AC21 portability rules after 180 days.

## Things worth doing early

- Keep a **personal file** of all I-20s, EADs, I-94s, approval notices, and pay stubs for every period of status. USCIS asks for them at each step.
- **Don't let status lapse.** 180+ days out of status can make adjustment of status very difficult.
- **Travel** during transitions is the classic way to blow up a timeline. Check with an attorney before international travel during cap-gap or with a pending I-485.
- **Maintain a second offer line**. The biggest H-1B risk is employer dependency; having a backup employer who will file a new petition dramatically reduces downside.

## Policy sensitivity

H-1B rules are politically volatile. Recent cycles have seen adjustments to wage-based lottery weighting, specialty occupation definitions, and processing fees. Assume the rules for 2027 will not be identical to the rules today; maintain a 6–12 month contingency runway.
